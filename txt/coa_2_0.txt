# COA 2.0 - Chart of Accuracy from Open Interest

## What COA 2.0 Means

COA 2.0, or Chart of Accuracy 2.0, is a market confirmation method based on
open interest behaviour. It studies call-side and put-side open interest, or
OI change, to understand whether the market is more likely to hold support,
hold resistance, break support, break resistance, or stay range bound.

This theory is taught inside the LTP Calculator course and is credited there
to Dr. Vinay Prakash Tiwari.

## Core Logic

- Call-side open interest acts like resistance because it tries to stop the
  market from moving upward.
- Put-side open interest acts like support because it tries to stop the market
  from moving downward.
- Each side can be in three states: increasing, stable, or decreasing.
- The three call-side states and three put-side states together create the
  nine scenarios of COA 2.0.

## OI Graph Setup on LTP Calculator

- COA 2.0 is read from the OI graph or OI change graph.
- On the LTP Calculator, the OI graph can be opened by clicking the OI change
  value on either side of a strike price.
- The graph shows call-side and put-side OI change from 9:15 AM up to the
  current market time.
- To choose the right strike, first identify the ATM strike price.
- A practical shortcut taught in the chapter is this:
  the strike where reversal values are visible on both the call side and put
  side is generally the ATM strike.
- The chapter explains the graph visually as:
  green line = call-side open interest
  red line = put-side open interest

## Nine COA 2.0 Scenarios

| Call OI state | Put OI state | Expected reading |
|---|---|---|
| Increasing | Increasing | Market can reverse from both sides; immediate support and immediate resistance both look strong; usually consolidation or range bound behaviour |
| Increasing | Stable | Immediate resistance is likely to hold, while immediate support may break down |
| Increasing | Decreasing | Immediate resistance is likely to hold, and immediate support is more likely to break down |
| Stable | Increasing | Immediate support is likely to hold, while immediate resistance may break out |
| Stable | Stable | Market can reverse from both sides; usually consolidation at that strike-price range |
| Stable | Decreasing | Immediate resistance is likely to hold, while immediate support may break down |
| Decreasing | Increasing | Immediate support is likely to hold, while immediate resistance may break out |
| Decreasing | Stable | Immediate support is likely to hold, while immediate resistance is more likely to break out |
| Decreasing | Decreasing | Market becomes unpredictable because both sides are weakening |

## Important Interpretation Rules

- Increasing call-side OI generally strengthens resistance.
- Increasing put-side OI generally strengthens support.
- Decreasing call-side OI weakens resistance and helps bullish breakout
  chances.
- Decreasing put-side OI weakens support and helps bearish breakdown chances.
- When both sides are stable or both sides are increasing, the market often
  behaves in a range-bound way near that strike zone.
- When both sides are decreasing, COA 2.0 becomes weak and the market can turn
  unreliable or noisy.

## Best Time to Use COA 2.0

- Prefer using COA 2.0 between 9:45 AM and 2:30 PM.
- Before 9:45 AM, new writer positions are still forming, so readings can be
  misleading.
- After 2:30 PM, writers often start squaring off positions, so the logic of
  COA 2.0 becomes less reliable.
- The chapter specifically warns that the double-decreasing scenario appears
  often after 2:30 PM, which is one reason to avoid relying on COA 2.0 late in
  the day.

## Where to Use COA 2.0

- COA 2.0 can be used during consolidation or range-bound conditions.
- It can also be used at diversions and at the end of diversions.
- Do not check COA 2.0 on the first hit at an extension.
- If the market comes again to the extension for a second hit, then COA 2.0
  can be checked for confirmation.

## Practical Trading Role

COA 2.0 is not just a definition chapter; it is a confirmation framework.
The idea is to look at how writers are behaving around the current ATM strike
and then decide whether support or resistance is likely to hold or break.

In plain language:

- Stronger call-side OI means bears are defending the upside.
- Stronger put-side OI means bulls are defending the downside.
- Weakening call-side OI means resistance is losing strength.
- Weakening put-side OI means support is losing strength.

## Summary

COA 2.0 is the open-interest-based confirmation system of the LTP Calculator
course. It combines the three states of call-side OI and the three states of
put-side OI to form nine scenarios. Those scenarios are then used to judge
whether the market is more likely to hold support, hold resistance, break out,
break down, or become unpredictable.
