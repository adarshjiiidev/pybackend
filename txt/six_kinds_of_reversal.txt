# Six Kinds of Reversal Theory

## Overview
There are SIX levels from which the market can reverse:
1. Resistance
2. Extension of Resistance (EOR)
3. Support
4. Extension of Support (EOS)
5. Diversion
6. End of Diversion

The market does NOT always reverse exactly at Support/Resistance — it often reverses from their EXTENSIONS.

---

## 1. Resistance
- Highest volume or highest open interest on the CALL side, closest to the imaginary line (starting from ATM pair, moving OTM)
- Resistance is always found on CALL SIDE

## 2. Extension of Resistance (EOR)
- The market doesn't reverse exactly at resistance; it extends a few extra points ABOVE resistance
- This extended level is called EOR
- How to find EOR: Click on the VOLUME of the resistance strike price on the AI LTP Calculator
- EOR = precise reversal price above resistance

## 3. Support
- Highest volume or highest open interest on the PUT side, closest to the imaginary line (starting from ATM pair, moving OTM)
- Support is always found on PUT SIDE

## 4. Extension of Support (EOS)
- The market doesn't reverse exactly at support; it extends a few extra points BELOW support
- This extended level is called EOS
- How to find EOS: Click on the VOLUME of the support strike price on the AI LTP Calculator
- EOS = precise reversal price below support

## 5. Diversion
- The market moves with a MOTIVE: from support to resistance (bullish) OR from resistance to support (bearish)
- Diversions are HURDLES in the market's path — mini support/resistance that can temporarily divert price
- Diversions cannot reverse the full trend but cause short-term pullbacks
- Diversions = levels to BOOK PROFITS on the way to the final target

### Identifying Diversions:
- If market motive is BULLISH (support → resistance): CALL side strikes between S/R = Diversions; PUT side = End of Diversions
- If market motive is BEARISH (resistance → support): PUT side strikes between S/R = Diversions; CALL side = End of Diversions
- Click on VOLUME of the strike to get the Diversion value

## 6. End of Diversion
- Each Diversion has an "End of Diversion" — the level from where market RESUMES toward its motive (target)
- At Diversion: book profits
- At End of Diversion: re-enter in trend direction

---

## Three Rules of Diversion

### Rule 1: Number of Diversions
Number of diversions = Number of strike prices BETWEEN support and resistance + 1

Example:
- Support at 17600, Resistance at 17700
- Strike prices between them: 17650 (just one)
- Number of diversions = 1 + 1 = 2

### Rule 2: No strikes between S/R → One diversion
- Support at 550, Resistance at 560
- No strike between them
- Number of diversions = 0 + 1 = 1

### Rule 3: S/R at same strike → No diversion
- Support = Resistance = 41500
- No difference between strike prices
- Number of diversions = 0
- Market trades strictly extension to extension (EOS to EOR)

---

## Summary: Where the Market Reverses (in order of frequency)
1. Extension of Support (EOS) — most common bullish reversal
2. Extension of Resistance (EOR) — most common bearish reversal
3. End of Diversion (support side) — for bearish pullbacks
4. End of Diversion (resistance side) — for bullish pullbacks
5. Diversion levels — short-term profit-booking points
