# Options Trading Concepts

## Definition
Options are derivative contracts that give the holder the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an underlying asset at a specified price (strike price) on or before a specified date (expiration date).

## Types of Options

### Call Option
- Right to **BUY** the underlying asset
- Profit when price goes UP
- Maximum loss: Premium paid
- Unlimited profit potential

### Put Option
- Right to **SELL** the underlying asset
- Profit when price goes DOWN
- Maximum loss: Premium paid
- Maximum profit: Strike price minus premium

## Key Terms

### Strike Price
The predetermined price at which the option can be exercised

### Premium
The price paid to buy an option contract
- Affected by: Time value, intrinsic value, volatility

### Expiration Date
The last date on which the option can be exercised
- Weekly, Monthly, Quarterly expiries available

### Open Interest (OI)
Total number of outstanding option contracts
- High OI = High liquidity
- OI buildup indicates strong support/resistance

### Greeks

**Delta**: Rate of change of option price with respect to underlying price
- Call delta: 0 to 1
- Put delta: 0 to -1

**Gamma**: Rate of change of delta
- Highest at ATM options

**Theta**: Time decay of option value
- Options lose value as expiration approaches
- Accelerates in final week

**Vega**: Sensitivity to volatility changes
- Higher volatility = Higher premium

**Rho**: Sensitivity to interest rate changes
- Less significant for short-term options

## Option Moneyness

### ITM (In The Money)
- Call: Spot price > Strike price
- Put: Spot price < Strike price
- Has intrinsic value

### ATM (At The Money)
- Spot price ≈ Strike price
- Highest Gamma
- Most liquid

### OTM (Out of The Money)
- Call: Spot price < Strike price
- Put: Spot price > Strike price
- Only time value, no intrinsic value
- Cheaper but riskier

## Option Strategies

### Directional Strategies

**Long Call**: Bullish strategy, limited risk, unlimited profit

**Long Put**: Bearish strategy, limited risk, limited profit

**Short Call**: Bearish/neutral, unlimited risk, limited profit

**Short Put**: Bullish/neutral, significant risk, limited profit

### Neutral Strategies

**Straddle**: Buy ATM call + ATM put
- Profit from big moves in either direction
- High cost due to double premium

**Strangle**: Buy OTM call + OTM put
- Cheaper than straddle
- Needs bigger move to profit

**Iron Condor**: Sell OTM call spread + OTM put spread
- Profit in range-bound market
- Limited risk and profit

**Butterfly**: Combines bull and bear spreads
- Profit when price stays near ATM

## Option Chain Analysis

### How to Read Option Chain
1. **Strike prices** in center column
2. **Calls** on left side
3. **Puts** on right side
4. **OI buildup** shows support/resistance
5. **Volume** shows current activity

### Key Indicators
- **PCR (Put-Call Ratio)**: OI(Put) / OI(Call)
  - > 1: Bullish sentiment
  - < 1: Bearish sentiment
  - > 1.5: Extremely bullish
  
- **Max Pain**: Strike with maximum OI
  - Price tends to move toward max pain near expiry

## Risk Management

1. **Never risk more than 2-5% per trade**
2. **Use stop losses** - Typically 30-50% of premium
3. **Don't hold till expiry** unless planned
4. **Avoid illiquid options** - Check OI and volume
5. **Beware of theta decay** in last 3-5 days

## Indian Options Market Specifics

### NIFTY Options
- Most liquid options in India
- Strike interval: 50 points
- Lot size: 25 (subject to change)
- Weekly + Monthly expiries

### Bank NIFTY Options
- High volatility, higher premium
- Strike interval: 100 points
- Lot size: 15 (subject to change)
- Wednesday weekly expiry + Monthly

### Stock Options
- Available for ~200 stocks
- Less liquid than index options
- Higher impact cost

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Buying deep OTM options (lottery trading)
2. Holding options till last day expecting miracle
3. Ignoring theta decay
4. Trading without understanding Greeks
5. Not using stop losses
6. Over-leveraging
7. Trading illiquid options

## Best Practices

1. Start with small positions
2. Paper trade first
3. Understand implied volatility
4. Use technical + OI analysis together
5. Have a clear entry and exit plan
6. Keep learning from trades
